How to use Arpeggio Flow

What does this app do?

It writes short arpeggio exercises for you. Pick a key and a chord progression, and it generates a fresh line of chord tones, shows it as sheet music, and marks where to play each note on the fretboard (or on the keyboard, in piano mode). Every exercise is new, so your ears and hands cannot run on autopilot.

Quick start

  1. Pick a key, a scale and a chord progression on the left.
  2. On guitar, pick a scale shape (where on the neck to play). On piano, pick the practice hand and its octave range.
  3. Click Generate Exercise.
  4. Click Play. The current note lights up on the diagram and the highlight follows the sheet, bar by bar.

What is the Guitar / Piano button?

It switches the whole app between instruments. The same generator drives both; only the diagram, the register and the notation change. The exercise on screen is regenerated for the new instrument.

How do I read the diagram?

Dots are the notes of the scale in the chosen position, with roots in green. While playing, the tones of the current chord stay bright and the rest fades. The small diagrams at the bottom show each chord of the progression on its own.

What do the rhythm options do?

"Notes per bar" trades quarter notes for pairs of eighths. "Time signature" switches between 4/4, 3/4 and 2/4. "Add rests" leaves a few beats silent, so you practice counting through them. "Turn around" picks where the line changes direction.

What plays along with me?

"Backing rhythm" adds a metronome click or a drum groove. "Backing chords" comps the chart's harmony under the exercise; in piano mode the comp is written on a second staff, like a left hand. "Ambience" adds room or echo to the lead sound.

What is Continuous shift?

With a shift on, every loop moves the exercise somewhere new: the next key, around the circle of fifths, or to the next shape or register. The chart previews where the loop goes next, so you can move early.

Where do my exercises go?

Every loop you play lands in History. Pin the ones you like and they survive a reload; "Cycle pinned exercises" plays the pinned list as a rotation. Export saves the exercise as a PNG, a PDF or a .json file, and Load brings a .json file back, ready to play.

No sound?

Check that Audio is ticked, then click Play again: browsers only allow sound after a click. The instrument sounds load from the network the first time you play.
